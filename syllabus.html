Course Description

The course deals with interactions between science and technology and social, cultural, political, and economic contexts that shape and are shaped by them. (CMO No. 20, series of 2013)

This interdisciplinary course engages students to confront the realities brought about by science and technology in society. Such realities pervade the personal, the public, and the global aspects of our living and are integral to human development. Scientific knowledge and technological development happen in the context of society with all its socio-political, cultural, economic, and philosophical underpinnings at play. This course seeks to instill reflective knowledge in the students so that they are able to live the good life and display ethical decision-making in the face of scientific and technological advancement.

This updated edition situates the course in the realities of the 2020s: the rise of artificial intelligence and generative AI, the spread of misinformation, disinformation, and deepfakes, breakthroughs in gene editing (CRISPR) and mRNA technology, the deepening climate crisis and the transition to renewable energy, data privacy and surveillance, and the emergence of the Philippines as a spacefaring and AI-adopting nation through the Philippine Space Agency (PhilSA) and the National AI Strategy.

Mandated TopicsThis course includes mandatory topics on climate change and environmental awareness (Chapter 11).
Companion Lecture SeriesThis syllabus is paired with a full set of chapter lecture discussions (Chapters 1–12), each with learning objectives, key terms, discussion activities, a self-check quiz, a 20-item HOTS test, a 20-item HOTS reinforcement assignment, and a 3-item futures essay — all matched to this outline.

Learning Outcomes

At the end of the course, the students should be able to:

Knowledge

  1. Articulate the impacts of science and technology on society, specifically Philippine society.
  2. Explain how science and technology affect society and the environment and their role in nation-building.
  3. Analyze the human condition in order to deeply reflect and express philosophical ramifications that are meaningful to the student as a part of society.
  4. Define and demonstrate the impact of social media, algorithms, and artificial intelligence on the students' lives and on Philippine society in general. Updated
  5. Evaluate the ethical, legal, and social implications of emerging technologies such as generative AI, gene editing, and autonomous systems. New

Values

  1. Imbibe the importance of science and technology in the preservation of the environment and the development of the Filipino nation.
  2. Critique human flourishing vis-à-vis the progress of science and technology such that the student may define for himself/herself the meaning of the good life.
  3. Foster the value of a healthy lifestyle toward the holistic and sustainable development of society and the environment.
  4. Value truth, intellectual honesty, and the responsible use of information and AI tools in an age of misinformation. New

Skills

  1. Creatively present the importance and contributions of science and technology to society.
  2. Examine shared concerns that make up the good life in order to come up with innovative and creative solutions to contemporary issues guided by ethical standards.
  3. Illustrate how social media and the information age impact their lives and their understanding of climate change.
  4. Practice digital and AI literacy: critically evaluate sources, detect misinformation and deepfakes, and use AI tools ethically and transparently. New

Course Policies New

Responsible Use of AI Tools

Consistent with the course's own subject matter, students may use generative AI tools only as aids for brainstorming, studying, and language polishing — and must disclose such use in every submission. Presenting AI-generated work as one's own analysis is a breach of academic integrity. Reflection papers, debates, and presentations must reflect the student's own reasoning and stance.

Academic Integrity and Information Ethics

All sources — print, online, and AI-assisted — must be properly cited. Sharing or amplifying unverified claims in course outputs is treated as a methodological error; students are expected to apply the fact-checking skills built in Chapter 8 across all coursework.

Inclusion and Environmental Responsibility

The class is a safe space for evidence-based disagreement. In keeping with the mandated environmental-awareness topic, submissions are paperless by default.
